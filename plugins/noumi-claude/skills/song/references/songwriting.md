# Songwriting decisions

Use this reference to make choices for the current song. It is a set of optional craft tools, not a required sequence, scoring formula, or complete-song template.

## Start from the task

First determine what this owner wants now; only then select a writing method. Current explicit requirements and direct feedback take priority over relevant sourced past preferences, which take priority over the selected artist's usual expression. A preference supplies context, not a universal formula. A song request without supplied lyrics needs no questionnaire or routine lyric approval. Fragments and iterative co-writing use the review boundary below.

For a new song, establish what is happening, who speaks, what they want or resist, and what changes. A celebratory chant, absurd comedy, lullaby, narrative folk song, and dense rap need different decisions. A story is optional: a groove-driven song can succeed through sound, repetition, and attitude.

For supplied lyrics, distinguish **verbatim use**, **limited revision**, and **free rewriting** from the request. Default to preservation. For continuation, read the existing voice and unresolved movement first; do not replace the user's writing with your own preferred imagery. For diagnosis only, explain the weakness and a possible local change without submitting audio.

## Collaborate without taking over

Complete original lyrics stay unchanged unless revision is requested; music generation must also be requested before submitting. When the owner provides only a few lines, preserve those lines within the agreed scope, complete the text, and show the full version for review before generating. An explicit earlier “complete it and generate directly” can authorize that path, provided the owner has not since asked to keep polishing or wait.

During multi-turn polishing, keep changes local to the requested section or layer and identify the current version. A revised text remains a draft until the owner confirms that version; do not infer permission to generate from praise or from an older confirmation. An edit after confirmation makes a new draft. Use the last version explicitly confirmed for generation, with no further “improvement” at submission. A new generation or paid retry needs authorization covering that spend.

Keep drafts and confirmed versions in the owner's permitted AI-host storage, not in a new Noumi online draft notebook. Local file writing requires permission; if nothing can persist, offer a copyable version and say so. Recover missing confirmed text rather than inventing it in a later session. See [Execution: version state and storage](execution.md#co-writing-and-lyric-version-state) for the exact submission and recovery rules, including the distinction between unsubmitted lyric drafts and generated Noumi DRAFT songs.

For songs about the owner's life, use only facts relevant to this work and ones they are willing to include. Host memories are context, not blanket permission to reveal personal or third-party details. Omit sensitive specifics unless their use is clearly authorized; do not invent unknown names, diagnoses, events, or relationships as real history. An explicit fictional premise may be developed as fiction. A personal song and a song for a different recipient also have different preference scopes.

Choose a working center: an address to someone, a small event, a contradiction, a refrain, a celebration, a question, or a sonic phrase. Generate distinct hook candidates privately if useful; select one that belongs to this song. Do not default every prompt to breakup nostalgia, rain, a cold drink, an old photograph, or a grand universal truth.

## Make sections do useful work

| Section concept | Possible job | Check the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verse | Tell or develop the story, usually with different lyrics on each return | Does it give the listener something meaningful to enter? A scene, claim, or rhythmic idea can also serve the verse. |
| Later verse | Add a consequence, changed detail, new event, or viewpoint | Does it advance or intentionally deepen the first verse? |
| Pre-chorus | Provide a transition leading into the chorus | Does its expectation or pressure help? Omit it if the song moves naturally without it. |
| Chorus | Return with the central message or memorable identity | Is the language memorable and singable for this genre? Images and direct speech both work. |
| Refrain | A repeated line, often at the end of a verse | A refrain need not be a separate full chorus. |
| Bridge | Create contrast or tension, potentially through a new perspective or sound | A bridge is not a synonym for pre-chorus. Omit redundant contrast. |
| Hook | The catchiest lyric phrase or melodic passage | A hook is a memorable function and can occur in different sections. |
| Outro | Release, leave a trace, return with altered meaning, or end decisively | Avoid summarizing a feeling already made clear unless the style benefits. |

These are musical/writing concepts. Consult the separate tag reference before converting them into literal model input. A hook is a function, not necessarily a `[Hook]` control tag. No section has a mandatory part of speech, line count, or proportion of “concrete” versus “abstract” words.

## Diagnose before rewriting

When the owner's feedback is vague ("it doesn't land," "too flat," "verse two goes nowhere"), locate the problem before changing words. These lenses draw on lyric-writing pedagogy, including Pat Pattison's work; use the one that fits. They do not authorize changing complete original lyrics or a confirmed final version without the owner's permission.

**Settled or open.** Decide whether this section's idea should feel resolved, such as a decision or arrival, or unresolved, such as a question, longing, or tension. Read it aloud if the host permits, or examine the phrasing; use the intended speaking tone as a clue. Then try making form and meaning agree. Text inspection does not establish how the generated music will sound.

**Four adjustable levers.** These are tendencies to test, not required counts or formulas:

- **Number of lines:** balanced groupings can feel settled; an odd grouping can leave something open. Melody and phrasing can reverse that effect, so do not automatically add a fourth line to every three-line section.
- **Line length:** a short line can punch; a longer line can flow; syntax that depends on the next line can pull forward.
- **Line rhythm:** regular phrasing can settle; an uneven or syncopated pattern can create movement. Consider key words on strong beats without claiming control of a melody that does not yet exist.
- **Rhyme arrangement and strength:** returning end rhyme can close; a delayed, absent, or looser rhyme can hold tension. Try perfect, family, added/removed-consonant, vowel-only, or consonant-only connection as relevant; see [Rhyme and singability](rhyme.md) for language-specific boundaries.

**Four points of view to consider early.** Ask who is speaking, to whom, and why. Direct address speaks to "you" and can work like a close-up. First-person narrative about someone else frames events through the speaker. Second-person narrative tells a story using "you" as its character; that is distinct from addressing a specific recipient. Third-person narrative gives a wider view. These are writing choices, not an exhaustive grammatical taxonomy. A deliberate viewpoint change may help; an accidental drift may obscure the song.

**Verse development.** Each verse can take the idea somewhere new: a new event, consequence, angle, or time. Rewording the same point with richer images may not fix a stalled verse. A repeated chorus takes meaning from the verse before it, so developing the verses can refresh the same chorus without rewriting it. If stuck, consider whether the current idea might make a useful second verse and what would naturally lead into it.

**Title angle.** If the title is open to revision, try who, what, when, where, why, and how, then consider more than the first obvious angle. This can help propose alternatives when a platform rule rejects a common title, but a rejection does not authorize replacing the owner's chosen title without agreement.

**Sense detail, when detail is missing.** Briefly explore one concrete object or moment using sight, sound, smell, touch, taste, the body's inner feeling, and movement; keep what serves this song. This is an optional exercise, not a seven-sense checklist for every line.

Change only the lever that matches the feedback and preserve what the owner approved. See [Pattison on prosody](https://online.berklee.edu/takenote/prosody-in-music-and-songwriting/) and [his point-of-view discussion](https://www.patpattison.com/pov) for source context, rather than treating this short diagnostic aid as the full theory.

## Select a technique for a specific weakness

| When the draft needs… | Try… | Avoid… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emotional credibility | A precise action instead of one generic adjective | Replacing every direct feeling with scenery |
| A distinctive world | One or two relevant sensory details | Decorating each line with all five senses |
| A relationship or time anchor | An object whose use or state changes | Arbitrary props borrowed from familiar hits |
| A fresh angle | A chosen speaker or a sustained viewpoint shift | Switching pronouns accidentally |
| Loss or distance | A trace, interruption, or meaningful absence | Hiding all essential context |
| Forward movement | A new event, consequence, or changed interpretation | Verse two that restates verse one with synonyms |
| Tension | Contrast between what is said and what is done | Contradictions that make the story incoherent |
| A surprising comparison | A metaphor from a relevant other domain | Mixing incompatible metaphor systems |
| Time or scale | A controlled jump between moments or distances | A montage the listener cannot orient within |
| Rhythmic emphasis | Parallel phrases with a meaningful final turn | Mechanically repeating the same sentence frame |
| Curiosity | A recoverable information gap and later reveal | A puzzle with no emotional payoff |
| A memorable return | Bring back a detail with changed meaning | Repeating the detail merely to tick a box |
| Human complexity | A believable self-contradiction or reluctant admission | Aphorisms presented as everyone's universal truth |
| Space | Leave something inferable unsaid | Ambiguity caused by omitted essentials |
| Humor or poignancy | Contrast between situation and reaction | Jokes that erase the requested tenderness |

Use a small number of techniques that solve the song's actual problem. Combining all of them usually reduces clarity. An image works when it carries an action, relationship, or emotional consequence; a direct statement works when its placement and voice make it earned.

## Original miniature transformations

These are local demonstrations, never a seed song or required vocabulary.

- Generic “I miss you” can become a changed habit: “I still leave room / on the narrow side of the bench.” Use only if the relationship and scene justify it.
- A verse can show a practical action; a refrain can express its meaning without explaining every symbol. A later verse can change the action rather than add unrelated scenery.
- Repetition can gain meaning when the surrounding event changes: the same “I'll be here” may begin as reassurance and return as a chosen boundary. It does not need a new slogan each time.

## Write for the mouth and the music

Read lines aloud at the intended pace. Place breaths where meaning naturally breaks. Avoid awkward word order created only for rhyme. Similar melodic phrases often benefit from compatible phrase lengths, but count sung syllables and stresses rather than enforcing equal printed characters.

For Chinese, keep natural word groups intact and consider whether the intended high or sustained notes have comfortable vowels. Printed tone patterns alone cannot predict the generated melody. For English and other stress-based languages, place important lexical stress plausibly on strong beats; do not treat character count as rhythm. For rap, groove and internal rhyme may matter more than tidy end rhyme; for chants or children's songs, short repeated lines may be intentional strengths.

Rhyme supports meaning, voice, and recall. It is optional and genre-dependent. Perfect rhyme throughout can be weaker than a natural phrase. Multilingual writing should have a meaningful reason for switching language and should preserve each language's natural phrasing.

## Revise within the agreed writing scope

Check the requested language and scope, a stable speaker, a discernible center, effective repetition, meaningful development if needed, singable phrasing, and alignment with the music. Remove an obvious cliché or incompatible image only when it improves this particular piece. Preserve useful roughness, wit, and direct emotion.

Apply these checks while drafting or during an authorized revision. They do not permit changes to complete original lyrics or the owner's confirmed final version. If a check suggests a change after confirmation, propose it as a new draft and wait for the relevant approval rather than silently polishing at submission.

Do not use a numerical text score as a quality guarantee. A lyric can read well and sing poorly, or read simply and become powerful through delivery. Actual melody, intelligibility, emotional performance, and mix require listening; text-only review cannot certify a “top-tier song.”

## Revise the layer the owner named

| Direct feedback | Appropriate local action | Preserve / do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The words work; the drums are too heavy.” | Keep lyrics unchanged; revise percussion density, weight, and mix in the music direction. | Do not replace the hook or infer a lifetime dislike of drums. |
| “Only the second verse feels vague.” | Add or replace a relevant action/detail in verse two within the allowed wording scope. | Keep approved sections and musical direction. |
| “The chorus doesn't land.” | First decide whether it should close or deliberately remain open; try one relevant change to line shape, rhythm, or rhyme strength. | Keep the approved theme and other sections; no automatic paid regeneration. |
| “Make this more direct, fewer metaphors.” | Replace the obscuring comparison with natural speech where it affects meaning. | Do not force imagery as a platform quality requirement. |
| “My child needs an easier chorus.” | Shorten phrases and use useful repetition for that recipient. | Do not update the owner's general taste to children's songs. |
| “I disliked this one.” | Use the feedback locally; if needed ask which layer mattered most. | Do not ban its genre, voice, or theme globally without a stated reason/scope. |

Use permitted host memory for explicit continuing preferences, recording the relevant source and scope rather than private story details. Without memory capability, keep feedback in this conversation and say so.
